Timeseries.

Healthcare Biometrics & Sequential Intelligence

Investigating dynamic physiological trajectories, stochastic early warning markers in intensive care, and non-stationary biological oscillations through mathematically grounded computational time-series modeling.

Theoretical Foundation

Why Time Series in Healthcare?

Dynamic latency, irregularly spaced observations, and time-dependent stochastic vulnerability in human physiology.

Clinical Context

Non-stationary physiological oscillators, never static vectors.

Traditional diagnostic paradigms evaluate patients as static records: single fasting glucose or isolated blood pressure. Homeostasis is governed by circadian biorhythms and autonomic regulation.

In intensive care telemetry and ambulatory monitors, the temporal derivative dY/dt and phase shifts convey early warning signals hours before decompensation occurs.

Methodological Evolution

From R Econometric Foundations to Python Neural Ensembles

Our research began in classical Box-Jenkins econometric frameworks in R (forecast, tseries) analyzing spectral density and stationary differencing.

We translated these statistical guarantees into high-throughput GPU pipelines in Python (PyTorch, Darts, statsmodels), backed by micro-latency asynchronous inference engines.

Classical: ARIMA, STL Decomp, ARCH
Transitional: Prophet, Gradient Boosted Lags
Modern Deep: TFT, N-BEATS, State-Space Mamba
